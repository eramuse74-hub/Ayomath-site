Instrumentation Foundations Trainer

mathematics and physics for the BME instrumentation chain
The spine: everything is rotation. Almost everything in year four reduces to rotation in the complex plane. Multiplication rotates and scales. $e^{j\theta}$ is rotation at constant rate. Trig identities are rotations composing. Impedance is resistance that rotates the phase. Second-order damping is where two roots sit in the plane. Resonance is driving in phase with the natural rotation. The Fourier transform winds a signal around a circle. One idea, eight costumes — and the figures on every module share one complex plane so you can feel that rather than be told it.

    How it works

    Every question has a four-level hint ladder instead of a solution. Level 1 asks an orienting question; level 2 points at the right representation; level 3 sets up the work and leaves one gap; level 4 is the full solution and only unlocks after you have attempted level 3. Wrong answers are matched against a catalogue of wrong models — the site tries to name the mental model behind the mistake, not the arithmetic.

    Each module has figures you drive with a slider or by dragging; the same figures appear inside the hint ladder at the moment they matter. After a helped solve you get three quick variants while the idea is hot, and the item comes back after three days and again after two weeks.

    This is not a course, a grade book or a tracker. Hours and module status live in the spreadsheet. This tracks question-level mastery, and nothing leaves your browser.
