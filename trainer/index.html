Instrumentation Foundations Trainer

mathematics and physics for the BME instrumentation chain
The spine: everything is rotation. Almost everything in year four reduces to rotation in the complex plane. Multiplication rotates and scales. $e^{j\theta}$ is rotation at constant rate. Trig identities are rotations composing. Impedance is resistance that rotates the phase. Second-order damping is where two roots sit in the plane. Resonance is driving in phase with the natural rotation. The Fourier transform winds a signal around a circle. One idea, eight costumes — and the figures on every module share one complex plane so you can feel that rather than be told it.

Modules

    How it works

    • Attempt first. Sketch, guess, commit. A wrong committed answer tells the site which wrong model you hold, and it answers that model — not the arithmetic.
    • Hints come one level at a time. Level 1 orients; level 2 points at the right representation; level 3 sets up the work and leaves one gap; level 4 is the full solution and unlocks only after you attempt level 3.
    • Figures you drive — sliders and dragging — sit on each module page and reappear inside the hints when they matter.
    • Quick drill: after a helped solve, three fresh variants while the idea is hot.
    • Schedule: a clean solve is mastered. A helped one returns in 3 days, then 14.
    • Progress lives in this browser only. Export a backup now and then; import it on another device.
    • Not a course, not a grade book. Hours and module status live in the spreadsheet; this tracks question-level mastery.
